    Connectors and main switch

    The ESC current only touches J1, the VIN copper, Q1–Q4, the VOUT copper and J2 on the plus side, and the GND plane on the minus side. Everything else on the board handles a few milliamps.

    AMASS XT60PW-M, male, right-angle
    Job
    Battery input. Pin 2 is battery plus (VIN), pin 1 battery minus (GND). It hangs over the left edge so the battery lead plugs in from the side.
    Rating
    30 A continuous, 60 A for under a minute, so the board is rated the same. Put a 40 A fuse in the battery plus lead: the board kills a hard short in under 0.5 ms, a long overload is the fuse's job.
    Why male
    The battery has the female XT60, so the board needs the male one to mate with it.
    AMASS XT60PW-F, female, right-angle
    Job
    Output to the ESC harness. Pin 2 is the switched plus (VOUT), pin 1 is GND, joined straight to J1 pin 1 by the bottom copper plane and its wire.
    Why female
    The live side of a power connection gets the shrouded female contacts, so nothing exposed carries battery voltage.
    Infineon IRFB3206, N-MOSFET, TO-220, 60 V, 3.0 mΩ max
    Job
    The switch itself: four in parallel on the plus side. The middle pin (drain) sits in the VIN copper, the right pin (source) in the VOUT copper, the left pin is the gate.
    Numbers
    Simulated drop 25.2 mV at 30 A (0.75 W for all four, 0.19 W each) and 50.2 mV at 60 A (3 W). No heatsink needed at these currents.
    Why N-channel
    An N-MOSFET has a much lower resistance than a P-MOSFET of the same size. Its gate must go above the battery voltage, which is what the floating 12 V supply (U3) does.
    Careful
    The metal tabs are the drains: they are at battery plus. Keep them off the hull and any metal at ground.
    10 Ω, 1/4 W, one per gate
    Job
    Each MOSFET gate gets its own small resistor from the shared gate bus FG. Without them, four parallel MOSFETs can ring against each other at turn-on and turn-off.
    Value
    10 Ω is small next to R35 (10 kΩ) that sets the charging speed, so it does not slow the switch down.
    1 MΩ, gate to source
    Job
    Keep each gate at 0 V when nothing drives it, for example with a cracked solder joint on the gate bus or before the supply is up. A floating gate could turn a MOSFET half on.
    Value
    1 MΩ takes only 13 µA from the gate supply.
    1.5KE33A TVS diode, 1500 W, 28.2 V standoff
    Job
    D1 sits across the battery side (VIN to GND), D2 across the ESC side (VOUT to GND). When 60 A is cut in a millisecond, the inductance of the battery and ESC leads kicks the voltage up; the TVS clamps it.
    Numbers
    Simulated battery-side peak when 60 A is killed: 17.6 V on 4S, 25.9 V on 6S. In a hard short while running: 21.2 V (4S) and 30.6 V (6S).
    Value
    28.2 V standoff: it does not conduct at a full 6S pack (25.2 V).
    0 Ω links
    Job
    Kelvin taps. RK1 takes VIN for the logic (net VIN_K) from one quiet point of the VIN copper; RK2 gives the gate supply its 0 V (net SRC) at VOUT. The pre-charge resistors also join VOUT there, on the bottom layer.
    Why
    The 30 A current never flows in the thin logic tracks, so the voltages the logic sees stay clean.

    Pre-charge

    ESCs carry large capacitors. Switching the battery straight onto them would pull hundreds of amps for a moment, enough to spark the XT60 and stress the MOSFETs. The board fills them through 11 Ω first.

    IRF9540N, P-MOSFET, TO-220, −100 V
    Job
    Switches the pre-charge path on: source at VIN, drain to F1. It carries 1.4–2.1 A for a fraction of a second at each start.
    47 kΩ · 22 kΩ · BZX55C12 zener 12 V
    Job
    Q5's gate. R1 holds it at VIN (off). When Q6 pulls R2 down, the gate goes to VIN × 22/69: gate to source −11.4 V on a full 4S pack.
    D3
    On 6S the divider would give −17 V; the 12 V zener stops it at −12 V, well inside the ±20 V limit.
    2N7000, N-MOSFET, TO-92
    Job
    Pulls Q5's gate down through R2 when PCEN (from the LM339) is high.
    Littelfuse RXEF050 PTC, 0.5 A hold, 72 V
    Job
    If the pre-charge stayed on into a fault, the PTC heats up within seconds and cuts the current to a trickle, so R3 and R4 do not burn.
    Value
    A normal pre-charge (1.4–2.1 A for about 0.1 s) is far too short to trip it.
    22 Ω 2 W each, in parallel = 11 Ω
    Job
    Limit the pre-charge current: simulated 1.38 A on 4S and 2.08 A on 6S. They join VOUT on the bottom layer at RK2.
    Energy
    Each start puts 0.43 J (4S) or 1.0 J (6S) into the pair; into a shorted output the LM339 stops it after 0.30 J. Each resistor takes a 1.5 J pulse.

    Floating gate drive

    The gates of Q1–Q4 must sit about 10–13 V above their sources, and the sources are at battery voltage when the switch is on. So the gate supply floats: its 0 V is VOUT.

    Mornsun B0512S-1WR3, 5 V → 12 V isolated, 1 W, SIP-4
    Job
    The gate supply. Its output 0 V (pin 3, net SRC) is tied to VOUT through RK2, so its +12 V (pin 4) is always about 12 V above the MOSFET sources, whatever the pack voltage.
    Numbers
    Unregulated: about 14.3 V at the light load here (simulated). Gates get 13.3 V.
    Check
    Pin order on the board: 1 GND, 2 Vin, 3 0 V, 4 +Vo. Check it against the datasheet of the module you buy.
    10 µF 16 V · 22 µF 25 V electrolytic
    Job
    C4 on U3's 5 V input, C5 on its floating output (V12F to SRC). The module switches internally and needs both.
    4.7 kΩ · 1N4744A zener 15 V 1 W
    Job
    R33 is a minimum load: unregulated modules climb well above 12 V with no load. D5 caps the floating supply at 15 V, below the MOSFETs' 20 V gate limit.
    PC817 optocoupler, DIP-4
    Job
    Its LED is on the 5 V side, lit by Q10 and Q11 through R32 (about 11 mA). Its transistor connects the floating 12 V to DRV. Only light crosses, so the 5 V logic never touches the floating supply.
    22 kΩ
    Job
    Pulls DRV down to SRC when U4 lets go. That is what turns Q12 on to empty the gates.
    1N4148 · 10 kΩ
    Job
    Feed the gate bus FG from DRV. R35 sets how fast the gates charge (about 1.4 mA, calculated). D10 lets DRV fall below FG at turn-off so Q12 can switch on.
    220 nF 100 V film · 100 Ω
    Job
    Soft start. C6 (FG to GND through R39) makes the gates rise slowly, so VOUT ramps up instead of jumping: 18.9 A peak when the MOSFETs close (simulated) instead of hundreds.
    R39
    Lets FG follow a sudden VOUT drop in a short instead of C6 holding the gates up: the simulated gate-to-source peak in a hard short is 15.9 V (4S) and 16.1 V (6S).
    Why film
    A film capacitor keeps its value at 25 V; a ceramic would lose much of it.
    P6KE18A TVS diode, 600 W
    Job
    Gate-to-source clamp. If VOUT falls in microseconds (a short), it keeps the gates within 18 V of the sources, under the 20 V limit.
    BC327 PNP · 47 Ω · 10 kΩ · 1N4148 · 1 kΩ
    Job
    Fast turn-off. When DRV falls, D6 and R38 pull Q12's base below its emitter (on FG). Q12 then dumps the gate charge into SRC through R36, and the four MOSFETs turn off together.
    R37
    Holds Q12 off while the gates are being driven.

    Sensors and arm chain

    Two different sensors in series: the reed passes 5 V on, and the Hall sensor lets Q7 pass it to ARM. A stuck Hall, a welded reed, a cut cable, a Hall without power or a shorted Hall wire still kill (all simulated, 1.53–1.59 ms).

    JST XH 3-pin · 100 Ω
    Job
    The KY-003 Hall module (1 = S, 2 = +, 3 = −). Its A3144 pulls S low when a south pole is near. R8 feeds it 5 V (net V5S) and limits the current if the cable is shorted.
    Check
    Measure the module's pin order: some clones swap + and −.
    JST XH 2-pin · 100 Ω
    Job
    The MC-38 reed door contact, closed by the magnet. R9 feeds it (about 3.5 mA, calculated) and limits a short in the cable.
    2N3906 PNP · 10 kΩ · 47 kΩ
    Job
    The second switch in series. The reed brings 5 V to Q7's emitter; Q7 turns on when the Hall pulls its base down through R10. R11 holds it off otherwise. So ARM is high only with the reed closed and the Hall on.
    4.7 kΩ
    Job
    Pulls ARM to 0 V as soon as either sensor lets go, so a cut cable reads as “magnet out”.
    100 kΩ · 10 µF 16 V
    Job
    Arm delay. EN charges through R15 and crosses 2.5 V about 0.8 s after the plug goes in (simulated: live after 816 ms). A plug still wobbling into place does not switch the ESCs on and off.
    2N3904 · 10 kΩ · 10 kΩ
    Job
    Inverter: NARM is high when ARM is low. R16 drives Q8's base from ARM, R17 pulls NARM up.
    2N3904 · 10 kΩ · 100 kΩ · 220 Ω
    Job
    Fast EN discharge. When NARM goes high, Q9 turns on and C7 empties through R20 (220 Ω × 10 µF = 2.2 ms time constant): EN is below 2.5 V about 1.3 ms after ARM drops, and the ESCs are off 1.6 ms after the magnet leaves.
    Why
    A slow fall would be a slow kill. A gap shorter than about 1 ms (vibration) is still ignored: 0.8 ms of chatter stays live in the simulation. R19 keeps Q9 off when its base is not driven.
    blue LEDs 3 mm · 1 kΩ each
    Job
    D7 (HALL) lights when the Hall sees the magnet, D8 (REED) when the reed is closed, about 2 mA each. Before a dive both must be on; one alone means a sensor is not seeing the magnet.

    Sequencing: one LM339

    Four comparators decide the order: pre-charge when armed, abort if VOUT cannot keep up, go live when VOUT has reached half of VIN, and reset everything when the magnet leaves.

    LM339N quad comparator, DIP-14 in a socket · 100 nF
    A
    Pins 4, 5 → 2: EN above REF lets PCEN rise (start pre-charge).
    B
    Pins 6, 7 → 1: VOD below RAMP pulls PCEN down (abort, or kill on a short while running).
    C
    Pins 8, 9 → 14: VOD above VIND raises DONE (VOUT is above about half of VIN).
    D
    Pins 10, 11 → 13: EN below REF pulls RRST down, which empties the ramp.
    Note
    Open-collector outputs: A and B share PCEN, either can pull it low. C3 decouples its 5 V.
    10 kΩ · 10 kΩ · 100 nF
    Job
    REF = half of 5 V = 2.5 V, for comparators A and D.
    75 kΩ 1 % · 10 kΩ 1 % · 470 kΩ · 10 nF
    Job
    VOD = VOUT ÷ 8.7 plus 0.09 V (from 5 V through R23), so at the first instant VOD sits just above a ramp that starts at 0 V. C9 filters it.
    160 kΩ 1 % · 10 kΩ 1 % · 10 nF
    Job
    VIND = VIN ÷ 17. DONE rises when VOD > VIND, which works out to VOUT above about half of VIN.
    1 MΩ · 68 kΩ · 3.3 µF X7R ceramic
    Job
    The ramp: once EN is up it rises toward VIN × 0.064 with a 0.21 s time constant (calculated). VOUT, charging through 11 Ω, must stay ahead of it. A shorted ESC bus cannot, so the pre-charge stops after 0.30 J and stays off until the magnet is pulled.
    Why ceramic
    An electrolytic's leakage current would upset a ramp fed through 1 MΩ.
    1 kΩ
    Job
    Comparator D empties C11 through it while EN is low, so every start begins from 0 V.
    10 kΩ
    Job
    Pull-ups for PCEN and DONE: the LM339 can only pull its outputs down.
    2N7000, in series
    Job
    An AND gate: CATH is pulled down only when PCEN (Q10) and DONE (Q11) are both high. Then U4's LED and the LIVE LED light and the gates turn on.
    red LED 3 mm · 1 kΩ
    Job
    LIVE: on when the ESCs are powered, about 3 mA. It shares CATH with U4's LED, so it shows what the gate drive is told.
    330 Ω
    Job
    U4's LED current, about 11 mA (calculated): plenty for the PC817 to drive the gate bus through 10 kΩ.

    5 V supply

    1N4007
    Job
    Blocks a reversed battery from the logic. (The power path has no reverse protection: the XT60 is keyed, keep it that way.)
    10 Ω · 47 µF 50 V
    Job
    Filter in front of the 7805: limits the surge into C1 at plug-in and smooths spikes on VIN.
    L7805CV, TO-220, lying flat, tab = GND
    Job
    5 V for everything on the logic side: sensors, LM339, LEDs and the B0512S.
    Heat
    On 6S it drops about 19 V. A K7805-500R3 or R-78E5.0-0.5 switching module fits the same holes and runs cool.
    10 µF 25 V
    Job
    The 7805's output capacitor.

    Wires, test pads, holes

    bare copper wire on three open strips
    Job
    The solder mask is left off three strips: VIN on top above the MOSFET pins, VOUT on top between the 1 MΩ and 10 Ω resistors, and GND underneath from the battery XT60 to the ESC XT60. A 12 AWG bare copper wire (or a thick layer of solder) on each carries most of the current, so the 2 oz copper does not have to.
    test pads: FG, EN, V5, GND
    Job
    For a meter or scope. FG shows the gate bus (about VOUT + 13 V when live), EN the arm delay and the kill.
    M3 mounting holes
    Job
    Mount the board on nylon screws and standoffs: the copper near the top holes is live.
